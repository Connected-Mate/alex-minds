The shift

You don't deploy AI. You become AI-native. Those are not the same project.

Today four rooms will tell you how to deploy. Tools, governance, copilots, agents. Mistral and BCG will sell it to you next door. I came to put a different question on the table. Becoming AI-native isn't a procurement problem. It's an identity problem. And only this room can solve it.

What's easy

Deploying AI

Procurement. Roadmaps. Vendor selection. Pilots. Change management programs. Adoption metrics.

A consultant can do this for you.

What's actually yours

Becoming AI-native

Redesigning who is in the room when a decision is made. Engineering senior–junior duos. Letting digital natives shape strategy, not just execute it.

No one outside this room can do this for you.

Three questions

Not mine to answer. Yours. Here they are — in the order I would put them on Wednesday's whiteboard.

01

Who's already living in 2030 inside Danske?

Not who's working on AI. Who, in your organization, is already using AI every day — to do their job, to learn, to decide. Name one. Under 30 if you can.

The pivot If you found one — is that person in this room? In the room where decisions are made? If not, it isn't a hiring problem. It's a representation problem.

For context: 76% of people under 30 use generative AI at work. For people 45+, 32%. Different worlds. Same office.

02

What duo would you trust on your hardest AI decision?

If you had to bet Danske's biggest AI decision of 2027 on one senior–junior duo inside your company — name them. Indivisible. Co-accountable. Two names on the project charter, not one.

The pivot If you can name one — what stops you from naming five more by year-end? If you can't name any — that's the work. The work is engineering the duos before you need them.
03

What decision could you take by Wednesday lunchtime?

Your offsite ends Wednesday at 12:45 with three to five priorities. Could one of them be: within twelve months, every ELT member has a coach under 30, and every transformation project has a named senior–junior duo at the top. One sentence. One commitment.

The pivot If yes — I've done my job. The rest is execution. If you hesitate — notice the hesitation isn't about AI. It's about changing who's in the room.

Closing

The four other rooms will tell you what to deploy. I came to tell you who to put next to you.

If you want to take this further

Three moves, designed for an institution that just left ten years of defence and is asking what to do next. None of them require a new strategy. They require a decision.

01

Reverse mentoring at the top

Every ELT member gets a coach under 30. Two hours a month minimum. Not training — conversation. Six months in, you'll be discussing different things at this table.

Already proven: Orange did it. 500 active duos. 5.8× ROI over 24 months. AI adoption among executives improved by 85%.

02

A shadow board of digital natives

Fifteen to twenty employees under 30 — yours, not consultants — with one job: challenge the ELT on a real project each quarter. Not advise. Challenge.

Already proven: Havas built a "COMEX of Digital Natives" — thirty-five young people whose official mandate is to push back.

03

Indivisible duos on every transformation

From now on, any project labelled "AI", "transformation" or "innovation" carries two names at the top. One senior, one digital native. Same recognition, same accountability. If you cannot find the digital native — that itself is a signal worth listening to.

The measurable upside (McKinsey): +27% likelihood of higher profitability, +19 points of innovation revenue for generationally diversified leadership.
